# Go-to-market for PayPrompt

## Positioning

> "You already did the work. PayPrompt sends the awkward email so you don't have to."

Not "invoice management for SMBs". One sentence, one job, one price.

## Where the first 10 customers come from

1. **r/freelance, r/entrepreneur, r/agency** - post the PROBLEM, not the product.
   "How do you chase overdue invoices without ruining the client relationship?"
   If 5 people answer "I hate this part", you have demand.
2. **Facebook groups** for local freelancers/designers/agencies (Algeria + France + Gulf).
3. **IndieHackers** - build-in-public log, they love micro-SaaS.
4. **Direct outreach** to 20 agencies with public "about us" pages and a visible client list.

## Pricing logic

Flat $19/mo beats per-invoice pricing: it removes the "should I bother?" decision
at the moment a freelancer is deciding whether to chase a $50 invoice.

## First 10 users, by hand

- Import their invoices manually (spreadsheet or screenshot).
- Configure their policy in 5 minutes.
- Give 2 months free for a testimonial + logo permission.
- Convert at month 3.

Chasing invoices manually for 10 people is the cheapest, most reliable market research
that exists, and it makes the product genuinely good before the first dollar.

## Numbers that matter

- 100 customers x $19 = $1,900 MRR
- 50 customers x $19 = $950 MRR (covers hosting, domain, Stripe fees)
- CAC target: < $50 (mostly time, not ads)
- Payback period: under 2 weeks at Pro pricing

## Content loop

Every week, post one real (anonymised) reminder sequence that worked:
subject line, what made it land, response rate. This is the marketing engine -
inboxes are the channel and the product proves itself in the same message.
